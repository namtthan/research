01 · About

Engineering microphysiological systems to understand the gut–microbiome axis

I'm a biomedical engineer working at the intersection of microfluidics, organ-on-a-chip technology, and host–microbiome biology. I develop physiologically relevant chip platforms that recreate intestinal microenvironments outside the body.

My work spans computational modeling, device engineering, bioinformatics and wet-lab biology—from chip design and fabrication through biological experiments and sequencing analysis.

Current work centers on patient-derived gut-on-a-chip and Crohn's disease models, probiotic and fecal microbiota interactions, epithelial barrier integrity, oxidative stress, mucosal inflammation, and radiation-induced intestinal injury.

02 · Research Focus

Human-relevant models, from device physics to disease biology

Organ-on-a-chip systems

Microfluidic gut-on-a-chip and multiplex organ-on-a-chip platforms for disease modeling and pharmaco-toxicology.

Host–microbiome interactions

Probiotic and patient-derived microbiota effects on epithelial oxidative stress, barrier function and inflammation.

Microfabrication

Porous PDMS membranes, photolithography, soft lithography and additive manufacturing for rapid device prototyping.

Fluid dynamics

Finite-element and computational modeling of flow, shear stress and transport in microfluidic systems.

Bioinformatics

Single-cell and bulk RNA-seq, 16S analysis and quantitative interpretation of engineered tissue systems.

Translational NAMs

Human-relevant preclinical models designed around mechanistic and decision-relevant endpoints.
